# The Timone Process

> The single written definition of the engineering process Timone enforces on every managed project. Stage skills implement this document; when a skill and this spec disagree, the spec wins and the skill gets fixed.
> Delivered by phase-01 for [PRD-01.R1](doc/specs/prd/prd-01-process-layer.criteria.md).

## Stage notes

**0 — Onboarding.** Registers a repo in `timone.yaml` (repo URL, path under `projects/`, stack, platform bindings), creates the artifact tree (`doc/specs/`, `doc/specs/prd/`, `doc/adr/`, `doc/plans/phases/`), and drafts the one-page product overview — problem, users, goals, success definition, non-goals, **and constraints** (client policies, hosting, compliance, budget). Stack choices are recorded as **founding ADRs**, never restated elsewhere. Onboarding also produces `doc/standards.md` — deliberately thin: which entries of Timone's central standards library apply, plus project-specific deviations; anything a tool can enforce lives in tool config, not prose. For an **existing** codebase, conventions are *observed* from the code, not imposed — conflicts with preferred standards are flagged for an explicit decision. Gate: human confirms the overview and the standards.

**1 — Triage.** Every incoming request enters here. Routing: feature → stage 2 (or 3 if requirements are already clear; when the request spans multiple independent unresolved decision areas, triage *recommends* stage 2's at-scale mode, `timone-wayfind` — the human decides); bug → stage 5, anchored on the criterion it breaks — ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0036](doc/adr/0036-feedback-is-triage-with-the-documents-open.md)), which retired stage 9; chore/technical enabler → stage 5, unanchored (see PRD anchoring below) — ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D3): **a chore meets no gate before its pull request.** It runs triage → stage 5 → stage 6 → a pull request unattended, skipping stages 2 and 3 as it always did and now meeting nothing that stops for an answer, so the human's judgement lands on the PR and nowhere earlier. **This is a gate a chore had and gave up deliberately** — fvermaut was asked whether a small chore should put a plan in front of him first or just get built, was told in the same breath that nothing then stops a misread chore before the work happens, and chose *just build it*; merging is still his, so nothing ships unreviewed. A reader finding a chore built without being asked about it is looking at the ruling, not at a defect: re-open ADR-0030 D3 before restoring anything. The triage record's rationale is therefore the last thing a human reads before the work starts, which is what makes the classification itself worth getting right; question → answered, no pipeline. **Triage reads before it decides, and decides on what it finds** ([ADR-0036](doc/adr/0036-feedback-is-triage-with-the-documents-open.md) D2). It opens the criteria register and the PRD narrative, the delivery and verification reports for the work the request touches, and the history of the files it names — what the request cites, and what is needed to judge it. It does not trawl the project for grievances nobody raised. This is what stage 9 used to do, and without it here the deep reading would simply be gone.

**The three kinds are defined by what the documents say, not by the words the reporter used** (ADR-0036 D3). A complaint that something is missing, slow or awkward where **no criterion promises it** is a `feature` — nothing is broken, the promise was never made, and this is the one most often filed as a bug. A complaint that the software does not do what a criterion **actually says**, quoted by name, is a `bug`; a rationale that cannot name the criterion is not describing one. A complaint that a committed document is **wrong** is a `chore`, and the document is what gets fixed. **A report filed as a bug that turns out to be a feature is a normal outcome and is what the reading is for** — including when it contradicts an earlier comment on the same ticket.

**Recording:** the classification (kind + entry point + one-paragraph rationale) is recorded on the request itself. When the request is a GitHub issue and the project's repo is GitHub-hosted, the record is an issue comment plus a `triage:<kind>` label (via `gh`). Otherwise — free-form request, or non-GitHub remote — it is persisted as `doc/triage/NNN-<slug>.md` in the target project (NNN zero-padded, allocated by scanning existing records) and committed; the fallback is always explicit, never a silent skip.

**2 — Requirements discovery.** Relentless interview: one question at a time, each with a recommended answer; anything answerable from the codebase is answered from the codebase, not asked. Ends with a summary of decisions and risks. Its conclusions are raw material for stage 3 — they evaporate unless persisted. The interview also maintains the project's **domain glossary** (`CONTEXT.md`, created lazily, glossary only — no implementation details): fuzzy terms are sharpened into canonical ones, conflicts with the existing glossary are challenged on the spot, and resolved terms are written down immediately.

**At scale — `timone-wayfind`.** When discovery exceeds one session — multiple independent decision areas, research needed between conversations, decisions blocking other decisions — stage 2 runs as **wayfinding** (adapted from Matt Pocock's wayfinder; [ADR-0010](doc/adr/0010-wayfinder-discovery-maps.md)): the idea is charted as a **map**, a single issue on the project's tracker labelled `wayfinder:map`, whose child issues are **decision tickets** — questions whose resolution is a decision, never slices of a build — each sized to one session and typed `research` (agent-alone), `grilling` (with the human — the default; the interview discipline above, glossary maintenance included), `prototype` (with the human — a cheap, throwaway artifact to react to when "how should it look/behave" is the key question; built via `timone-prototype` under the convention of [ADR-0011](doc/adr/0011-prototype-convention.md)) or `task` (manual work that unblocks a decision, never a deliverable — anything that is a build routes through stage 1 like any other request). Charting names the **destination** first, tickets what is already sharp, and writes the rest as fog (**Not yet specified**); working sessions claim one frontier ticket (open, unblocked, unassigned), resolve it, record the resolution on the ticket, and graduate whatever fog the answer sharpened — **one ticket per session**, `research` excepted. **Every ticket a human is expected to act on carries its CTA in its own body**, worded for its type ([ADR-0022](doc/adr/0022-a-conversation-ticket-can-be-answered-in-writing.md)): `grilling` and `task` offer both ways to answer — write it on the ticket, or run the takeover; `prototype` offers the takeover alone, since there is nothing to react to until it is built; `research` says no action is needed. A ticket carrying a question and no instruction is a defect, not a terse ticket — it was the state `ivtrends` was found in on 2026-08-09, and it is what the rule exists to prevent. **The map is scaffolding, never spec:** nothing on it is normative — a resolution passing stage 4's significance test becomes an ADR at decision time, the destination lands in-repo (normally the stage-3 PRD pair), and PRDs and ADRs restate what they need rather than point into tickets, keeping ADR-0006 intact. A project whose repo is not GitHub-hosted falls back — loudly, as in stage 1 — to a committed markdown map under `doc/wayfinder/`. The map closes only after the destination artifact is committed, its closing comment linking it.

**3 — Requirements.** **The artifact comes first, then the gate** ([ADR-0014](doc/adr/0014-artifact-first-gates.md)): the pair is written on a work branch with the narrative stamped `Draft`, and the human approves *that*, not a paraphrase of it in conversation. Approval flips it to `Active`, and that flip is the gate's written trace. A change request re-enters this stage, which rewrites the pair in place. **Requirement IDs are fluid until the gate closes** and stable forever after it — a rejected draft is renumbered on rewrite rather than leaving deprecated blocks for requirements nobody ratified. The PRD pair is the **single source of truth** for what is being built (ADR-0006): a stakeholder-readable narrative free of test machinery, and a formal criteria register. Tickets scope work and point here; they never hold requirement detail. PRDs covering user-facing functionality include accessibility acceptance criteria derived from the mandatory baseline (see "The standards library"). **Prototype-assisted approval** (optional; offered when the work has a user-facing surface): the approval conversation may run against a running throwaway prototype (`timone-prototype`, [ADR-0011](doc/adr/0011-prototype-convention.md)) — the human reacts to a preview URL while the agent maps what they see to requirement IDs and folds reactions into the list. The gate itself is unchanged: approval lands on the requirement list, never on the prototype, and every divergence between prototype and list is surfaced explicitly before approval is requested — a prototype under-specifies by nature (error paths, priorities, non-UI and accessibility criteria).

**4 — Architecture.** ADRs record decisions passing the three-part significance test — **hard to reverse**, **surprising without context**, and **the result of a real trade-off**; if any part is missing, no ADR. They are standalone artifacts created the moment the decision is made — in a grill session, during planning, or mid-execution — **never scheduled as plan work**. Format: Status / Date / Context / Decision / Consequences. Superseding creates a new ADR and flips the old one's status with a cross-link; history is never edited. Reference examples: Timone's own `doc/adr/0001`–`0006`.

**5 — Planning.** A phase is a coherent, end-to-end-testable increment cut into thin vertical slices (schema → API → UI, not layer-by-layer), each sub-phase independently executable by a fresh-context sub-agent with copy-pasteable validation commands. Docs last; seed data second-to-last. Requirements map at phase level, never per sub-phase. Each sub-phase declares its **seams under test** — the public boundaries its tests will observe behaviour at — agreed at planning time; implementation writes tests only at declared seams. Prefactoring comes first when it makes the change easy ("make the change easy, then make the easy change"). Wide mechanical refactors (blast radius across the codebase) are the sanctioned exception to vertical slicing: sequence them as **expand–contract** (add the new form beside the old → migrate call sites in batches → delete the old form). **Required elements:** a phase file carries the requirement IDs it delivers (per the phase-level mapping rule above) and, per sub-phase, file markers naming every file created or modified, an explicit dependency statement, the declared seams under test, and copy-pasteable validation commands with the assertions they must satisfy. Sub-phases carrying no code say so rather than omitting the seams silently. The document layout that carries these elements belongs to the plan skill, not to this spec. **PRD anchoring:** feature phases list the requirement IDs they deliver; technical/enabler phases are explicitly stamped un-anchored, naming what they deliver and why they are not PRD-bound. ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D3) — **that stamp is written, not agreed in advance.** It used to need human agreement sought at this stage's gate; a chore reaches planning ungated and meets nothing that stops for an answer before its pull request, so there is no longer a moment at which that agreement could be collected. The judgement moves to the pull request, where the stamp sits in front of the human alongside the code it justifies. ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0038](doc/adr/0038-a-screens-shell-is-built-before-its-behaviours.md)) — **a phase that builds or substantially changes a user-facing screen opens with a shell sub-phase, and this stage identifies which screens those are.** The shell delivers the screen's stylesheet (from the kept prototype per ADR-0037, or the project's design file where no prototype settled it), its layout, regions, density, figure treatment and column sizing, and enough static content to render — a screen a person can look at, not a stylesheet nobody has seen — carrying the accessibility obligations from its first commit, since those are layout decisions and retrofitting them is what forces a later slice to restyle. Every other slice depends on it, fills it in using its classes, and **may not restyle**: a slice finding the shell wrong amends the shell as a marked plan amendment rather than overriding it locally, because local overrides accumulating across contexts are individually reasonable and collectively the defect. A phase with no screen has no shell slice and is not defective for it. **ADR gate:** if planned work implies a significant undocumented decision, stop and record the ADR before the plan is written. **The breakdown, and what it gates:** ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D1) — an initiative driven by a ticket is first cut into **pieces**, one pull request's worth of work each, listed at `doc/plans/breakdowns/ticket-NN.md` and written once. **That list is this stage's one gate:** it is written on the work branch stamped `Awaiting approval`, the human approves the committed file, and the stamp becomes `Approved by <who> <date> — N pieces` — the count being what later shows whether the list has grown since they saw it. **[ADR-0014](doc/adr/0014-artifact-first-gates.md) is unchanged in substance:** the artifact is still written first and the gate is still taken against the committed file; only which document is in front of the human moved. Approving it also merges chunk zero without a pull request (D2), and the file is immutable afterwards, never written back into the file (D4). ✏ Revised 2026-08-20 ([ADR-0040](doc/adr/0040-one-step-is-one-ticket-and-doneness-is-a-fact-about-a-ticket.md)) — **approving it also opens one ticket per step, and the initiative's ticket becomes a map of those children**, exactly as a wayfinder map is for its decision tickets. The gate itself does not move: the committed file is still the one thing the human approves, still stamped with its piece count, and a list that gains a step is still a re-proposal. **Which step is next is no longer derived from a count.** It is the first step ticket that is open, unblocked and unassigned — wayfinding's frontier rule, applied to the build — so doneness is a fact about a ticket rather than an arithmetic over runs. A step ticket closes when its own pull request merges; the initiative's ticket closes when every one of its children has. This retired settledness outright ([ADR-0029](doc/adr/0029-a-chunk-advances-only-on-success.md), superseded): a count of settled runs drifts from the work at every cancellation and restart, silently, which is how `ivtrends` #1 came to believe a piece was built that never was. `TERMINAL` is untouched and keeps its own separate job. **Two shapes of work have no breakdown by design and are not defective for it:** a chore or technical enabler, which triage routes straight to a phase file (D3), and hand-run work with no driving ticket. **Status lifecycle:** the phase file carries a `Status` line from birth, and ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 (ADR-0030 D1) it is a **lifecycle marker, not the written trace of a gate** — `Planned` when the plan skill writes it, and `Complete — see <report>` at phase close, which is the state stage 6's outcome check reads. Nobody approves a phase file and nothing waits on one. **Amendments:** a committed plan is amended in place, never silently rewritten — every change made after it was committed carries a dated `✏ Refined <date>:` marker so execution can see what moved, and sub-phases added mid-phase take the next free letter with the same marker. **Re-approval:** ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D1) — a phase file carries no approval, so an amendment to one voids nothing and needs no re-approval however much it grows: what the human agreed to is the piece, and its pull request is where they judge what it turned into. What *can* go stale is the **breakdown** — a list that has gained a piece since its own stamp was written is a re-proposal, and the piece nobody has seen is not executable until the human approves the list again. Scope growing beyond an amendment is a new phase, not a bigger one; a post-delivery plan change is a fresh request through stage 1.

**6 — Implementation.** The orchestrator decomposes the phase into per-sub-phase sub-agent runs — fresh context each, only the relevant plan excerpt, prior handoff, and file list. Sequential by default; parallel only for slices sharing zero files. Within a sub-phase the loop is **TDD, red before green**: write a failing test at a declared seam, run it red, implement just enough to go green, repeat — one slice at a time, no speculative features. Anti-patterns are rejected: implementation-coupled tests (break on refactor without behaviour change), tautological assertions (expected value recomputed the code's way), and horizontal slicing (all tests first — bulk tests verify imagined behaviour). Refactoring is deferred to the delivery review **for identification**, not to the red→green loop — stage 8 raises it and it is filed as a `chore` through stage 1; delivery itself never commits code, since a refactor landing after the verification report would invalidate that report's evidence at the moment it is presented. Type-check regularly, run single test files during the loop, the full suite once at the end. A sub-phase is complete only when its validation checklist passes. ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0039](doc/adr/0039-the-look-is-gated-twice.md)) — **a shell sub-phase ([ADR-0038](doc/adr/0038-a-screens-shell-is-built-before-its-behaviours.md)) does not close until a fresh context that did not build it opens the shell and its reference side by side at the same window size and reports the differences**, each of which is fixed or written down with its reason — the reflow and accessibility adaptations ADR-0037 requires being the expected content of that list. It is a **named comparison, never a pixel diff**: density, how figures are set, how columns are sized, ink levels, signal colours, the treatment of any frozen region; pixel comparison against a deliberately adapted screen would fail always and mean nothing. An unexplained difference fails that slice's validation like any other failing check, under the bounded retries below. Bounded retries (max 2 per sub-phase), then escalate to the human. **Entry gate:** ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D1) — **what execution refuses to start without is agreement, and what carries agreement is the breakdown, not the phase file.** A ticket-driven piece is executable when its initiative's breakdown is stamped `Approved` and names that piece; a breakdown still `Awaiting approval`, or one whose list has grown past the count its own stamp carries, is not — execution refuses, routing to the human when no breakdown was ever approved (re-planning cannot manufacture a missing approval) and to stage 5 when a re-proposal is what left the approval stale. The two shapes of work stage 5 gives no breakdown — a chore, whose judgement moves to its pull request (D3), and hand-run work with no driving ticket, agreed by the human who invoked it — are **not** refused for its absence. The phase file's `Status` line gates nothing: it is a lifecycle marker, and the only state this stage writes on it is `Complete — see <report>` at phase close. **Work branch and commits:** ✏ Revised 2026-08-05 ([ADR-0015](doc/adr/0015-branch-per-driving-unit.md)) — **the branch belongs to the driving unit of work, and execution never cuts a new branch when an approved plan already lives on one.** Ticket-driven work owns its `timone/<n>-<slug>` branch from the requirements stage until its pull request merges or closes: requirements, plan, code, reports and the PR head are one branch, and the artifact commits execution finds there are expected, never divergence. Hand-run work with no driving ticket owns one branch per phase, named `phase-NN-<slug>`, cut from the project's default branch — or, when the previous phase is complete but still **unmerged**, stacked on that phase's branch instead and said so in the completion report, since merging is stage 8's and a human's, so a completed phase awaiting review is the normal state and a rule demanding the default branch would mean no project could ever start its second phase (stacking arises only on this path — a ticket-driven run holds its project until its PR reaches a terminal state, so the next ticket's branch always cuts from a current default branch). A ticket that grows into multiple phases is deliberately undecided (ADR-0015). Execution refuses to start on a dirty working tree, or on a branch carrying commits that are neither the driving unit's own artifacts nor its resume trace. **Dirty** means tracked modifications or untracked files that are not the phase's own artifacts: the phase file, the handoff file, the completion report and the ordinary products of a dependency install are never the dirt that blocks a start, since the escalation path deliberately leaves in-flight work behind and an escalated phase must remain resumable. One commit per sub-phase, made only **after** that sub-phase's validation passes, messaged `<type>: NNx — <deliverable>` — so `git log` alone answers which slices actually landed. The phase-close write (completion report plus the `Status` flip) is not a sub-phase and carries no `NNx`: `docs: close phase NN — <theme>`, mirroring stage 5's `docs: plan phase NN — <theme>`. **Handoff notes** carry what the slice built, the files it touched, decisions taken inside the slice, its validation evidence including the red-green trace, and anything the next slice must know; they are appended to `doc/plans/phases/reports/phase-NN-handoffs.md`, one section per sub-phase, committed with that sub-phase's commit. **Completion report** (`doc/plans/phases/reports/phase-NN-complete.md`) **required elements:** the date, a link to the plan and — ✏ Revised 2026-08-15 ([ADR-0030](doc/adr/0030-the-breakdown-is-a-stage-and-chunk-zero-merges-without-a-pull-request.md) D1) — to the **approved breakdown the piece was agreed in**, since a phase file no longer carries an approval trace of its own; where there is no breakdown the report says so explicitly, which is the correct state for a chore (D3) and for hand-run work with no driving ticket, the requirement IDs delivered and their resulting statuses, a per-sub-phase outcome table carrying commit SHAs, deviations from the plan, and context for the next agent. **Escalation, concretely:** after two failed attempts at a sub-phase's validation, execution stops, starts no dependent sub-phase, and reports the failing validation step with both attempts; because commits happen only after validation passes, the branch is left at the last passing sub-phase and the failing work stays uncommitted in the working tree — the escalation says so rather than leaving the state to inference. As in stage 5, the document layout carrying these elements belongs to the execute skill, not to this spec.

**7 — Verification.** A verifier context that did **not** watch the build checks observable behaviour against the criteria register — trusting only the running app, never source-code intent. Channels: `api` (terminal-checkable, forms the standing regression suite), `browser`, `human` (both reported as HUMAN-CHECK with a precise manual script). **Verdicts:** PASS / FAIL / HUMAN-CHECK / BLOCKED. REGRESSION is the FAIL variant reserved for a criterion that entered the pass already `verified` — the same failure class (it counts against the gate's zero-regressions clause and consumes the same fix loop), a distinct label because it means shipped behaviour broke, and stage 1 reads it differently. BLOCKED means the check could not run at all — environment down, tooling absent, criterion unverifiable as written; it asserts nothing about behaviour, leaves the register untouched, blocks the gate, routes to the human, and consumes no fix loop. **Independence:** the verifier reads from a closed list — the criteria register(s), the PRD narrative, the phase file's `Status` and requirements header (never sub-phase bodies), the completion report whole, a prior verification report's HUMAN-CHECK-scripts section alone (and only that section — the obligation to re-issue a carried-forward script verbatim cannot otherwise be discharged from the list, and a stage-7 artifact carries evidence, never build knowledge), the project's `README.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, `doc/standards.md` and `STATUS.md` (which stage 7 is obliged to update), the operational configuration standing the app up rather than implementing it — compose files, `.env`/`.env.example`, the run and test scripts a package manifest declares — and Timone's standards baseline — and the report declares what was read. Expected behaviour comes only from the register; the completion report supplies operational facts (how to run the app, carried-forward HUMAN-CHECKs), never expected values. Never read: handoff notes, diffs or `git show` of code, anything under the source tree including the committed test suite, and ADRs. The project's own suite may run once as a build-health smoke, reported as such, but is never criterion evidence — evidence comes from verifier-authored throwaway probes, in scratch space, never committed. **Scope:** a pass covers the requirement IDs the phase claims plus the standing regression set — every criterion with priority MUST, verify-via `api` and status `verified`, computed from the register at verify time (derived, never maintained as a second artifact); each report lists the derived set so the computation audits. For an un-anchored phase the claimed set is empty by design: the pass is the regression set plus carried-forward HUMAN-CHECK items from the completion report, and the gate degenerates to zero regressions — stage 6's validation already covered the un-anchored deliverable itself. **Environment:** verification observes the deliverable in its production form where the stack distinguishes one — a production build, never the dev server. Browser-channel checks for user-facing deliverables include the baseline accessibility checks (automated scan where tooling exists, HUMAN-CHECK script otherwise); scan violations are failures. **Fix loops:** the verifier never fixes — fixing means reading source, and a verifier that has read the fix has build knowledge for the re-verify. Each FAIL produces a defect brief — criterion ID, expected per the register, observed behaviour, reproduction commands, evidence — and a fresh fix context implements and commits `fix: verify NN — <criterion-id> <slug>` on the phase's branch; the verifier ingests only the returned commit SHA, never the fix transcript. One loop is all of a pass's FAILs briefed and fixed followed by one full re-verify of everything except already-scripted HUMAN-CHECKs. Max 2 loops after the initial pass, then remaining failures are recorded with evidence, the register flips to `failed`, and the work goes to the human via stage 1. **Register writes:** only this stage writes the register's **verdict** statuses — `draft` / `verified` / `failed`, the evidence-driven ones — once per pass, at pass conclusion, in the same commit as the report, `docs: verify phase NN — <theme>` on the phase's branch; the report-flip coupling in one commit is the evidence link, and the register line itself stays bare. The register's **intent** transitions (`revised`, `DEPRECATED`) are the one carve-out: they are **stage 3's**, made in the same commit as the PRD amendment that motivates them (✏ 2026-08-19: stage 9's until [ADR-0036](doc/adr/0036-feedback-is-triage-with-the-documents-open.md) retired it — the PRD's owner inherits them), and they assert nothing about behaviour — a `revised` criterion returns here for a fresh verdict. **A `revised` line re-enters exactly as a `draft` one does** — stage 3 sets it when intent moves, and the next pass writes a fresh verdict on the new wording: a criterion whose text changed has no evidence until it is re-checked, and a requirement gaining a clause loses `verified` as a whole, since a requirement's status is the weakest of its clauses' outcomes. `draft` or `revised` → `verified` on PASS; `draft` or `revised` → `failed` at loop exhaustion; `verified` → `failed` on an unresolved REGRESSION, with a dated ✏ marker naming the report; a requirement carrying an unperformed HUMAN-CHECK clause stays `draft` with a dated partial-evidence marker linking the report and the script — a requirement's status is the weakest of its clauses' outcomes. **Verification report required elements:** the date and scope statement (or the un-anchored stamp), a verdict summary table (ID / priority / channel / verdict / loop), the environment and an independence declaration naming what was read, per-criterion evidence with commands as run and per-clause outcomes, HUMAN-CHECK scripts verbatim, a regression section stated even when empty, fix-loop accounting, register changes, and a handed-to-the-human section whenever anything remains. Re-verifying an already-reported phase appends an iteration section to the existing report, never a second file. As in stages 5 and 6, the document layout carrying these elements belongs to the verify skill, not to this spec.

**8 — Delivery.** Delivery presents finished work for human judgement; it does not improve it, does not merge it, and decides nothing the pull request exists to let a human decide. **Entry gate:** a phase stamped `Complete — see <report>` whose verification report exists and whose stage-7 gate passed — every MUST criterion PASS or HUMAN-CHECK, zero unresolved regressions, within 2 fix loops. A phase `Complete` with no verification report refuses and routes to stage 7; any register status of `failed`, or any BLOCKED verdict, refuses and is filed as a ticket for stage 1, since a failed pass has already spent its loops. **An unperformed HUMAN-CHECK does not block delivery** — it is carried into the PR body as an explicit, unticked checklist item naming where its script lives, because merging is a human act and the PR is where that human already is; its requirement stays `draft`, and delivery never writes the criteria register. ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0039](doc/adr/0039-the-look-is-gated-twice.md)) — **a phase carrying a user-facing screen does not get its pull request opened until the human has seen the built screen beside its reference and said yes**, and the delivery report records that they did. This is a judgement, not a measurement, and it is the judgement the pull request exists to obtain: presenting a screen its owner has not seen is not presenting it. It reverses nothing about HUMAN-CHECKs — a scripted accessibility check is evidence gathered later and still never blocks — and a "no" is not a review finding: it re-enters stage 1 like any other rejection. **Gate order is input resolution → phase `Complete` → verification gate → the look gate → platform binding:** gates about the work precede gates about where it goes, so an unverified phase hears that rather than a complaint about its host.

Before the PR opens, a **two-axis review** runs as parallel fresh-context sub-agents, each with its own read list. **Standards** reads the phase's diff, the project's `doc/standards.md`, the code-smell review reference, and the project's tool configuration — the last so it can *skip* what linting, formatting and type-checking already enforce; repo standards override the smell reference on conflict. **Spec** reads the diff, the PRD pair, and the phase file's requirements header, and asks whether the diff faithfully implements the PRD, flagging missing requirements, scope creep, and wrong-looking implementations. Neither axis reads the other's report, and **neither reads the verification report** — behaviour evidence is stage 7's, and a spec reviewer holding a PASS table reviews the report instead of the diff. The axes are reported separately, never merged into one ranked list: standards-clean code can build the wrong thing, and spec-faithful code can break conventions. **The reviews report; they never block, and they never refactor.** Findings do not withhold the PR — a review that withheld it would hide its own findings from the only person who can act on them. Refactoring identified at the delivery review is raised in the Standards report and filed as a `chore` through stage 1: code committed after the verification report and before the human's read would invalidate stage 7's evidence at the moment it is being presented.

**Both reports are committed artifacts**, not PR prose alone (ADR-0006 — the repo is the source of truth; a PR body lives on a platform): `doc/plans/phases/reports/phase-NN-delivery.md` carries both axes verbatim, the PR URL, and the scope and verification summary the PR body quotes, committed on the phase's branch as `docs: deliver phase NN — <theme>` **before** the PR opens. Work then lands as a pull request. **Required elements:** a title following the phase's convention; the driving ticket reference, or the requirement IDs when the project has no ticket home; a scope summary; the verification outcome as a verdict table, with outstanding HUMAN-CHECKs as unticked checklist items; both review reports under distinct headings, Spec findings quoting requirement IDs; a link to the committed delivery report; and the base branch with its reason whenever it is not the default branch. **Stacked branches:** when the phase's branch was cut from a previous phase's unmerged branch (stage 6's stacking rule), the PR is opened against *that parent branch*, not the default branch, and the body says which PR must merge first — delivery never merges and never rebases, so merge order is the human's. **Platform binding:** per ADR-0004 the PR *is* this stage's artifact, so a project whose `repo_url` is not GitHub-hosted is refused loudly and routed to the human — unlike stage 1's triage record there is no doc-record fallback, because a delivery record without a review surface would mean shipping nothing while reporting success; `gh` absent or unauthenticated refuses the same way. **Re-delivery updates, never forks:** a branch that already has an open PR gets that PR's body refreshed and an iteration section appended to the existing delivery report — never a second PR, never a second file. Merging is a human act (or an explicitly configured auto-merge policy, later). As in stages 5 to 7, the document layout carrying these elements — the PR body, the delivery report, the shape shared by both review sections — belongs to the deliver skill, not to this spec.

**9 — Feedback. ✏ Retired 2026-08-19** ([ADR-0036](doc/adr/0036-feedback-is-triage-with-the-documents-open.md)). There is no stage 9. A reaction to work already delivered is a request like any other and enters at **stage 1**, which reads the project's documents before it classifies and routes on what it finds.

**Why it went.** Stage 9 and stage 1 did the same job — classify, route, build nothing, write a record, stop for the human — and differed only in that one opened the criteria register and the other read a ticket. Its three layers were stage 1's three kinds seen by a reader who had opened the documents: *the intent changed* is a **feature**, *what was built is not what was agreed* is a **bug**, *the record is wrong* is a **chore**. It was built, gated live on `scratch-app` [#4](https://github.com/fvermaut/scratch-app/issues/4), and its one contribution there was to overturn a triage decision made from a ticket's text alone.

**What moved to stage 1** is the reading and the definitions — see stage 1's note. **What did not move** is the carve-out: a concrete change-requesting review comment on an open pull request is already the human's confirmation and rides the verify-fix shape through remediation ([ADR-0016](doc/adr/0016-review-remediation-rides-the-verify-fix-shape.md)), as it always did.

**The intakes that are not tickets** — a stage-7 pass that ran out of loops or reported BLOCKED, a criteria line left at `failed`, the outcome of a performed HUMAN-CHECK — **are filed as tickets and triaged like anything else.** One front door, and it costs a ticket.

**Records already under `doc/feedback/` stay** as history. New ones are triage records under `doc/triage/`.
**10 — Deployment / 11 — Maintenance.** Stages defined so the process is complete end-to-end; their skills are post-MVP (see PRD-01 out-of-scope). Deployment releases a merged state to a target environment via the project's platform bindings. Maintenance turns dependency updates and production issues into triaged intake for stage 1.

## The standards library

Timone hosts the central standards library under `standards/` in its own repo, in three tiers:

- **Baseline (mandatory, all projects, no opt-out):** UI/UX guidelines and **accessibility** — a legal requirement under the European Accessibility Act (Directive (EU) 2019/882, applicable since 2025-06-28); working baseline EN 301 549 / WCAG 2.1 AA. Every project's `doc/standards.md` includes the baseline unconditionally. Enforcement points: user-facing PRD requirements carry accessibility acceptance criteria (stage 3), browser-channel verification includes accessibility checks (stage 7), and the delivery Standards review covers the baseline (stage 8).
- **Stack entries (selected per project):** TypeScript, Next.js, Prisma/PostgreSQL, better-auth, testing, Docker Compose local, Vercel + Supabase live, …
- **Review references (applied by stage 8 to every project, overridable):** the code-smell reference the delivery Standards review checks a diff against. A project's own `doc/standards.md` overrides it on conflict — which is why this is not the mandatory baseline, a tier that admits no exceptions. This tier is also the sanctioned exception to the "true of every project on Earth" rule below: a review checklist shared across projects is universal by construction, and a per-project smell list would make two reviews incomparable.

Content is authored and reviewed by the human, versioned centrally, injected into sessions like the stage skills. Discipline: tool-enforceable rules live in tool config (the eslint/tsconfig/prettier setup *is* the standard); library entries capture only what tooling cannot enforce; a line that would be true of every project on Earth doesn't belong. Each project's `doc/standards.md` = baseline + selected entries + recorded deviations — central evolution, project-level exceptions (the baseline admits none).

## Cross-cutting utilities

Not lifecycle stages of a feature — these apply across sessions and phases rather than within one pass through stages 0–11.

**Status reporting.** No owning skill — an obligation on **every** stage. Whenever a stage completes work, it updates `STATUS.md` (at the managed project's root, or Timone's root for Timone's own phases) so the human can see where things stand without reading a phase file. Plain language, no unglossed process jargon, and it always says **which** repository an item belongs to — the commonest confusion is not knowing whether the next action sits on a managed project or on Timone itself. It states what is done, what is in progress, what happens next, and what is waiting on the human. It is written **for** the human and is never an agent's source of truth: the PRD, phase files and reports remain the authorities, and `STATUS.md` must be regenerable from them without loss. A stage that changes a project's state and leaves this file stale has not finished. Requirement: [PRD-01.R22](doc/specs/prd/prd-01-process-layer.criteria.md).

**It is written only on the project's default branch — never on a work branch.** Every stage rewrites the whole file, and unlike the phase-scoped reports (one file per phase, so two phases never touch the same one) there is only ever one `STATUS.md`. Two work branches that both update it conflict the moment the second one merges, and a merge conflict in the artifact whose entire purpose is to tell the human where things stand is the worst possible place to have one. It is also where the human reads: the default branch is the one they open. **Mechanics:** the stage commits it on the default branch as its own commit — `docs: STATUS.md — <theme>` — **pushes that commit to `origin`**, and then **returns the clone to the branch it was on**; a stage that leaves the clone somewhere other than it found it has not finished. **Accepted consequence:** the file therefore describes work that is not yet merged, so every item names which branch and which pull request it belongs to. That naming is not optional — it is what makes a default-branch file able to describe in-flight work at all.

**Every stage pushes what it commits, and the push is part of the obligation, not a courtesy afterwards.** This applies to the whole of a stage's output — the artifacts on a work branch as much as `STATUS.md` on the default branch. A commit that exists only in the local clone is invisible to the human this file is written for, invisible to any other machine, and lost with the working copy; "committed" and "delivered to the reader" are not the same claim, and the process must not let a stage report the second while having done only the first. Stage 8 pushes today only because `gh pr create` refuses to open a pull request for an unpushed branch — an accident of one tool, not a rule, and no other stage has that accident to protect it. **The failure mode this closes is silent and asymmetric:** it never breaks anything locally, so the stage that commits without pushing looks exactly like a stage that finished, and the gap surfaces only when someone else — or the same person on another machine — goes looking for work that was reported done. It has been observed twice: stage 9's first live intake committed four feedback records nobody could open (2026-08-02, corrected in the skill), and a verification and a delivery pass in the same session each died between their commit and their push, leaving a report on disk and nothing on the remote. **A stage that has committed and not pushed has not finished**, and a stage interrupted between the two has stopped at an incomplete boundary rather than a clean one.

**Handover.** Owning skill: `timone-handover`. Purpose: capture the full state of an in-progress body of work — what's done, what's in flight, key decisions since the last handover, and the exact next action — so a fresh session or a different agent can resume without re-deriving context from the conversation. Two scopes: **meta** (Timone itself — which phases/PRDs are done, what's queued; artifact: `doc/handover/<date>-<slug>.md` in Timone's own repo) and **project** (one managed project's ticket/feature in flight; artifact: `projects/<name>/doc/handover/<date>-<slug>.md`). A handover doc references artifacts by path — PRDs, ADRs, phase files, reports — it never restates their content. Superseded handovers are kept, not deleted (same discipline as ADRs): the newest file for a scope is the current one.

**Prototyping.** Owning skill: `timone-prototype`. A prototype is a cheap, throwaway artifact built to be **reacted to rather than read**, answering one stated question — used by stage 2 at scale (a `prototype` ticket raising the fidelity of one open decision) and stage 3 (prototype-assisted approval), one convention for both ([ADR-0011](doc/adr/0011-prototype-convention.md)). It lives on a `prototype/NN-<slug>` branch in the project repo so the Docker preview machinery (ADR-0005) can serve it clickable — the human must be able to click it; a screenshot is not a prototype. It is **never merged and never the base of a work branch** — delivery refuses it, execution may not start from it. ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0037](doc/adr/0037-a-prototype-that-settles-a-look-is-kept-and-only-its-presentation-crosses.md)) — **the cherry-pick fence splits: presentation crosses, behaviour does not.** A prototype that settled how a screen *looks* hands its **stylesheet** to the build, which adapts it; markup, logic and fixtures still never cross. Adaptation is not a copy — what the accessibility baseline and 320 px reflow require is rebuilt, and every rule that had to change is recorded with its reason, an unrecorded change being drift. The exemption below waives nothing still: the PRD's accessibility criteria outrank anything the stylesheet brought with it. Fidelity is the cheapest that answers the question (static mock with fake data up to stubbed real-stack code), sized to one session. What survives is the human's reaction, recorded where the question lives — the ticket, or the requirement list under approval; the branch is deleted when the effort it served closes — ✏ Revised 2026-08-19 ([ADR-0037](doc/adr/0037-a-prototype-that-settles-a-look-is-kept-and-only-its-presentation-crosses.md)) **except one that settled a user-facing look, which is kept and stays servable indefinitely, and whose deletion is a defect.** Keeping is not merging: the fence above is unchanged. A project's **first** kept prototype is harvested once into a committed **design file** — ground, ink levels, row heights, how a figure is set, how a column is sized, signal colours — so a screen with a prototype matches its prototype and a screen without one matches the file; without that harvest a product looks like two products, which is the state `ivtrends` was found in. Prototypes are exempt from the accessibility baseline (they ship to nobody), and an approved inaccessible prototype waives nothing — the PRD's accessibility criteria stand.

## Stable requirement IDs

- Format `PRD-NN.R<k>`. **The clock starts at approval** ([ADR-0014](doc/adr/0014-artifact-first-gates.md)): before stage 3's gate closes, a rejected draft is renumbered freely on rewrite, since nothing has been ratified and there is nothing for stability to protect. From approval on, IDs are stable forever: never renumbered, never reused, never deleted.
- Intent changed → same ID, criteria updated, `Status: revised`, dated marker.
- No longer applies → `DEPRECATED` with a one-line reason; the block stays.
- New need → next unused ID.
- Every MUST has at least one Given/When/Then criterion and a verification hint; a MUST that can't be written testably gets sharpened with the human or downgraded.

## Gates, conversations and the human

Every stage's closing gate is either mechanical (validations pass) or human (a decision or review). Human interaction comes in two kinds, each with its own medium ([ADR-0012](doc/adr/0012-conversation-channels.md)):

- **Gates** are single decisions with one CTA — approve a PRD, approve a plan, confirm/decline/defer a feedback item. A gate decision is expressed as a **ticket or PR reply, the sole write-path for decisions**; a chat channel may notify, deep-link, or later relay a decision, but the relay posts it onto the ticket, and the loop reads one surface.
- **Conversations** are multi-turn interviews — grilling, wayfinder sessions, prototype walk-throughs. They run on a **conversation channel** behind one adapter seam: a chat application, or the universal fallback, a **terminal takeover** — the ticket carries a CTA naming a `timone takeover` command that resolves what the ticket is waiting on and spawns the right stage session. An acceptance that *concludes* a conversation (accepting the grill summary) is an in-conversation act; the session posts the accepted outcome to the ticket as the record. Transcripts are not process artifacts and nothing may cite them; truth lands in the PRD, ADRs and `CONTEXT.md` as always.

  ✏ Revised 2026-08-09 ([ADR-0022](doc/adr/0022-a-conversation-ticket-can-be-answered-in-writing.md)) — **a ticket waiting on a conversation names two ways to answer it, and the human picks without explaining the choice: write the answer on the ticket, or run the takeover.** Both reach the same session and produce the same record. **The written path is real, not a courtesy:** the daemon watches conversation-waiting tickets and spawns the session that ingests the answer, so writing it causes something to happen. **A human comment after the machine's question is the answer** — no keyword, no syntax, read exactly as a gate reply is. **Bounded at one clarifying round:** an answer that settles the question resolves the ticket; one that is partial or ambiguous gets what is still open posted back once, and if that still does not settle it the session says the rest needs the takeover, because a written path that never escalates is the comment ping-pong ADR-0012 struck out. **The wording follows what the ticket is waiting for:** a question the human can type an answer to offers both paths; one whose object does not exist yet — a prototype nobody has built — offers the takeover alone; one that asks the human for nothing says so.

- **A handoff is a wait, not a failure** ✏ Added 2026-08-16 ([ADR-0031](doc/adr/0031-a-handoff-is-a-wait-not-a-failure.md)). A stage that stops part-way and asks the human for something **parks its run on them**, carrying the same `conversation` wait as any other question — so both answer paths above apply unchanged, and the reply re-enters **the stage that asked**, because that stage is the one that has to judge whether the answer settles anything. A failure is a different event and keeps its own road: a stage that ended without an outcome, or claimed work it did not do, fails and is re-armed with `timone retry`. **A parked handoff holds its project until it is answered**, as a pull request awaiting review does; `timone cancel` is the way out of one nobody wants to answer.
- **Every command a ticket names can be run while the daemon is running** ✏ Added 2026-08-16 ([ADR-0032](doc/adr/0032-a-human-command-asks-the-daemon-to-act.md)). A human command that needs the run ledger while the daemon holds it **asks the daemon to act** — the daemon stays the ledger's single writer ([ADR-0023](doc/adr/0023-one-answer-one-session.md)) and carries the request out on its next pass, and the command reports what happened rather than that it asked. Nothing in the process may instruct a human to stop the daemon first, and no call to action may name a command the machine would refuse.

**The harness routes; the human never does.** The human is assumed to know nothing about this process. Every request — ticket, comment, or raw terminal prompt — is classified and routed through stage 1 by the harness, and no surface ever requires the human to name a stage, a skill, or a process concept. This binds interactive sessions today exactly as it binds the daemon: a session receiving a raw request about a managed project runs triage first and invokes the routed skill itself.

**Every message to a human ends with a CTA** — one explicit, visually set-off statement (its own line or bullet) of what the human is being asked to do. A message that asks nothing says so ("no action needed"). How the rest of that message is written is [Writing to the human](#writing-to-the-human) below.

**Every human wait is a session boundary** ([ADR-0013](doc/adr/0013-stateless-session-reentry.md)): sessions are never held open across a gate or an unanswered CTA; resumption re-enters statelessly from the committed artifacts and the ticket thread.

The daemon orchestrates stage skills — it never reimplements them.

## Writing to the human

✏ Added 2026-08-16. Normative, and it binds every surface a person reads: a ticket body, a ticket comment, a pull request body, `STATUS.md`, terminal output, and what a session says in a chat window. It binds sessions and the daemon alike. It was written after fvermaut read Timone's comments on `scratch-app` #31 — several hundred words each, restating requirements he had already agreed — and said they were too long to use.

**Write for someone who does not know this process and does not read English as a first language.** Short sentences. Common words. Never the rare word where a plain one works — write *established*, not *bought*. Never process vocabulary: no stage numbers, no skill names, no *criteria register*, *chunk*, *gate*, *un-anchored*, *frontier*. If a word only makes sense to someone who has read this file, it does not go in front of a human.

**A ticket is for the conversation, not for the specification.** It carries three things and nothing else: what is being done, how far it has got, and what is needed from the human. Requirements, acceptance criteria, schemas, file contents, command output and technical design never appear on a ticket. They live in committed artifacts, and the ticket links to them. This is ADR-0006 read from the human's side: tickets scope work and point at the repo, and the repo holds the truth.

**A comment is a few sentences.** Aim under 150 words. Anything longer is a draft that has not been cut yet — cut it before posting, do not post it and apologise for the length. Structure: one bold line saying where things stand, a short paragraph, the links, then the CTA.

**Asking the human to decide is the one thing that may carry its reasoning, and it carries it short.** Keep the recommendation, one sentence of why, and one sentence of what it costs. That is the conversation itself, so it stays on the ticket rather than moving to a file the human then has to open. Everything behind those two sentences is a link. *(Decided by fvermaut on 2026-08-16, over the alternative of moving the reasoning into a committed note.)*

**Do not restate what the human already told you.** A one-line confirmation that their answer was recorded is enough; the full list belongs in the artifact that now holds it.

**Do not report what the human cannot act on.** ✏ Added 2026-08-18 ([ADR-0034](doc/adr/0034-a-technical-stop-is-retried-not-reported.md)). The machine's own machinery breaking — a dropped connection, a service that is down, a refused login — is not the ticket's business while the machine still has a way through it. It tries again first, quietly, and speaks only once it has run out of ways. When it does speak, it says the fault is its own, says what it tried, and asks for nothing. Written after `ivtrends` #1 was stopped three times in one day by errors that had nothing to do with the work, each time with a message telling the human something had gone wrong.

**Every message ends with a CTA**, per the rule above. "No action needed" is a CTA and must be written out.
