# ADR-0030: The breakdown is a stage of its own, and chunk zero merges without a pull request

- **Status:** proposed
- **Date:** 2026-08-15
- **Source:** [phase 22](../plans/phases/phase-22.md)'s `✂ Cut` of 2026-08-15, which stopped 22c on two questions the plan could not answer from inside a slice and named them as the next phase's to settle up front
- **Extends:** [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md), whose D1 said the planning stage "gains a second artifact and a second gate shape" without deciding the mechanism, and whose D2 said approving the breakdown merges chunk zero without deciding what performs the merge

**This record is `proposed`, and it is the only one in this directory that is.** Every other ADR here writes down a ruling fvermaut had already made; this one proposes a resolution that *follows from* rulings he has made — [ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md)'s rhythm and [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md)'s five decisions — and he has not seen it. **Approving the phase that implements it is what accepts it**, exactly as approving [phase 22](../plans/phases/phase-22.md) was the ruling that [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md) recorded. Until then, nothing below is normative and the mechanism is still open.

## Context

[ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md) split the ticket from the run and chose the approval rhythm: **the breakdown once, then each pull request.** [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md) then said what a breakdown *is* — `doc/plans/breakdowns/ticket-NN.md`, a committed artifact, "written by the planning stage" (D1) — and that the requirements stage and the breakdown share one branch, chunk zero, which approving the breakdown merges (D2).

**Neither said how the machine is shaped to do it, and phase 22 broke on exactly that.** 22a and 22b landed the ledger half — chunked run ids, settledness, `timone cancel`. 22c, 22d, 22e and 22f were **cut unbuilt** by fvermaut on 2026-08-15 ([the cut is recorded in place](../plans/phases/phase-22.md#-cut-2026-08-15--22c-22d-22e-and-22f-are-not-built-and-are-not-this-phases-any-more), and the [completion report](../plans/phases/reports/phase-22-complete.md) closes the phase against its reduced scope). The cut names three reasons, and two of them are questions rather than missing file grants:

- **The pipeline's shape is ambiguous in the plan itself.** D1 has the *planning* stage writing the breakdown; D2 has it sharing a branch with *requirements*; the slice's file markers had the gate *after* `requirements` opening on it. That reads as either a new `breakdown` stage or the existing requirements gate changing its artifact — "different builds, and a slice context holds strictly less than the planner did, so it must not be the one to choose."
- **D2 has no home.** *"Approving the breakdown merges chunk zero"* is behaviour nothing in the daemon has. Verified: no source file merges a branch. `src/git.ts` exports `clone`, `isGitRepo`, `isClean`, `currentBranch`, `defaultBranch`, `fetch` and `fastForward` and nothing else — its only `git merge` is `merge --ff-only @{u}` at [`git.ts:104`](../../src/git.ts), which pulls a branch onto its own upstream. Merging work into a default branch is a thing the daemon has never done, and [`prompts.ts:429`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts) tells the delivery session in terms: **"Never merge — merging is the human's act."**

Those two are what this record settles, so that the phase which re-plans the breakdown starts with the gate machinery scoped rather than discovering it mid-slice. It does not re-argue [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md); it takes the shape that decision fixed and says how the machine carries it.

## Decision

**D1 — The breakdown is a pipeline stage of its own, `breakdown`, sitting between `requirements` and `planning`.**

- `breakdown` declares `waits: "gate"`, runs **once per initiative**, writes `doc/plans/breakdowns/ticket-NN.md` stamped `Awaiting approval`, and its approval both stamps the breakdown and merges chunk zero (D2 below). Its `next` is `planning`.
- `planning` stops gating — `waits: "none"` — runs **once per chunk**, and writes that chunk's phase file as an artifact with no gate of its own.

**The load-bearing argument is the shape of the graph, not a preference about naming.** A stage declares exactly one `waits` value in [`pipeline.ts`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)'s `STAGES` record ([`:181`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)), through the `waits: WaitKind` field of `StageFacts` ([`:95`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)), and every reader asks for it through the single accessor `waitFor` ([`:438`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)). [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md)'s D1 has the planning stage writing the breakdown; its consequences keep each chunk getting a phase file of its own. **One `planning` stage doing both would have to be gated on its first visit and ungated on every later one** — a stage whose meaning depends on how many times the ticket has entered it, which `waits` cannot express and `readGate` ([`:503`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) would have to be taught to ask about. Splitting them gives each stage one job and one wait, and leaves `waitFor` a fact rather than a computation.

**The objection, answered rather than dodged: D1 says the breakdown is written by *"the planning stage"*, so a `breakdown` stage looks like a contradiction of an accepted record.** It is not, because the two senses of "stage" are already distinct in the code. Each `STAGES` entry carries a **`processStage`** field — "the stage of `process.md` this is, for anyone comparing the two" ([`pipeline.ts:93`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) — and the mapping has never been one-to-one. **Four pipeline stages are already `processStage: 2`**: `clarification` ([`:196`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)), `wayfinding` ([`:215`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)), `charting` ([`:235`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) and `research` ([`:256`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)); `remediation` and `feedback` are both `processStage: 9` ([`:328`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts), [`:338`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)). `requirements` is `processStage: 3` ([`:273`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) and `planning` is `processStage: 5` ([`:283`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)).

**So `breakdown` is declared `processStage: 5`.** It is then written by the planning stage in the sense D1 means — `process.md` stage 5, the stage the `timone-plan` skill implements — while being its own step in the machine. The precedent is not invented for this: [ADR-0024](0024-every-open-ticket-answers-for-itself.md) already split `charting` off `wayfinding` inside one process stage for the same class of reason, that two different waits cannot live in one row.

**A second thing this shape buys, and it is the piece the cut sub-phase 22d needed:** it gives chunk *N+1* a clean re-entry point. When a chunk's pull request merges and the breakdown still lists a chunk, the ticket re-enters at `planning` — ungated, writes the next phase file, builds. Without the split there is no stage a later chunk can enter without re-opening a gate the human has already answered.

**The alternative, named with its cost:** *keep the existing `requirements` gate and change the artifact it opens over*, while `planning` stops gating. Fewer moving parts, no new row in the graph, no new prompt. **Rejected on two counts.** It puts the PRD and the breakdown under one approval, so the human answers `approve` to two artifacts at once — which muddies [ADR-0014](0014-artifact-first-gates.md)'s whole point that the human approves *the artifact* and not a paraphrase or a bundle. And it makes the *requirements* stage write the breakdown, which contradicts [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md)'s D1 outright instead of reconciling with it.

**D2 — On approval of the breakdown, the daemon merges chunk zero's branch into the default branch directly. No pull request.**

- The merge happens where the approval is already handled: beside `recordApproval` ([`session.ts:1036`](../../src/daemon/session.ts), called from [`:469`](../../src/daemon/session.ts)), after the stamp is committed and pushed, and before the run advances to `planning`.
- A merge that fails leaves the run failed with the reason on the ticket, exactly as a failed approval record does today ([`session.ts:1063`](../../src/daemon/session.ts)) — chunk 1 must not cut from a default branch that does not carry the specification.

**The argument is the rhythm.** [ADR-0028](0028-the-breakdown-is-an-artifact-and-the-ticket-follows-it.md)'s D2 states it: the rhythm is *"the breakdown once, then each pull request"*, and approving the breakdown *"is one gesture with two effects, and is not a third touch"*. A pull request for chunk zero would be that third touch — the human approves the breakdown, then is asked to merge the thing they just approved — and the rhythm is the decision [ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md) chose over both alternatives it weighed. **And there is nothing for the review to do:** chunk zero carries the PRD pair and the breakdown, both of which the human has just read and approved in the gate. A pull request exists to invite a review of code; the review has already happened, on the artifacts, at the gate.

**The cost, named:** this is **the first place the daemon writes to a default branch without a pull request**, and it is the first merge it performs at all. [ADR-0015](0015-branch-per-driving-unit.md) does not forbid it and does not licence it — read for this, it speaks only of a work branch that "owns its `timone/<n>-<slug>` branch from the moment the requirements stage claims it until the pull request merges or closes", and its stacking clause reasons that "the next ticket's branch always cuts from a default branch that already contains (or has declined) the previous work". **Chunk zero is the first branch whose life ends somewhere other than a pull request**, so [ADR-0015](0015-branch-per-driving-unit.md)'s ticket-path sentence acquires an exception, and the phase implementing this must amend it there rather than leave the reader to infer it. [ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md) already moved that sentence from the ticket to the chunk; this moves its endpoint for exactly one chunk.

**The alternative, named with its cost:** *open a pull request for chunk zero and let the human merge it*. It is uniform — every branch ends at a pull request, [ADR-0015](0015-branch-per-driving-unit.md) needs no amendment, `prompts.ts`'s "never merge" stands untouched, and the daemon needs no new git primitive. **Rejected:** it is the third touch the rhythm was chosen to avoid, and it asks for a review of a document the same human approved minutes earlier through a different channel — which is not a second opinion, it is the same opinion collected twice.

## Consequences

- **A new pipeline stage is a wider edit than it looks, and every site below was checked.** `breakdown` joins `PIPELINE_STAGES` ([`pipeline.ts:66`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) and `STAGES` ([`:181`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)) with a declared model and effort — [`pipeline.test.ts:326`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.test.ts) requires one of every built, unattended stage. It joins `PROMPTED_STAGES` ([`prompts.ts:15`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) and needs a `stageBody` case ([`prompts.ts:324`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) — **that one is compiler-enforced**: `stageBody` returns `string` and its switch has no `default`, so an uncovered member fails the build with TS2366. It needs a prompt of its own beside `planningPrompt` ([`prompts.ts:876`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)), and an `APPROVAL_RECORD` row ([`prompts.ts:599`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) saying what the breakdown's stamp is — while `planning`'s row ([`prompts.ts:606`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) goes, along with `planningPrompt`'s `Awaiting approval` instruction ([`prompts.ts:897`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) and `executionPrompt`'s claim ([`prompts.ts:562`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)) that the phase file's own `Status:` line "is the authority on whether you may build it".
- **The gate comment moves.** A `breakdown` row joins `GATED` ([`gate-comment.ts:87`](../../src/daemon/gate-comment.ts)) and `planning`'s row ([`gate-comment.ts:96`](../../src/daemon/gate-comment.ts)) is removed. Note that `GATED` is a `Partial` record, so **neither change is a compile error** — a stage that should gate and has no row simply posts nothing, silently. This is one to assert in a test rather than trust to the type.
- **Two deliberately-exhaustive tests fail by design, and that is the intended alarm.** [`pipeline.test.ts:407`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.test.ts) asserts the gated set is exactly `["requirements", "planning"]`; it becomes `["requirements", "breakdown"]`. [`cta.test.ts:327`](../../src/daemon/cta.test.ts)'s `WAIT_AT` is a total `Record<PipelineStage, …>` written out by hand *"rather than read back from `waitFor()`"*, guarded by a key-set assertion at [`cta.test.ts:348`](../../src/daemon/cta.test.ts); it needs a `breakdown: "gate"` row and `planning` flipped to `undefined`. **Both are working exactly as written** — they exist so a new stage cannot slip past unnoticed — and updating them is part of this decision, not evidence against it.
- **`STAGE_LABELS` ([`status.ts:19`](../../src/commands/status.ts)) is optional polish, not blast radius.** It is a `Partial` record and neither `requirements` nor `planning` has a row today; an unlabelled stage falls back to its own name ([`status.ts:82`](../../src/commands/status.ts)), and "breakdown" reads acceptably. Worth a row for tone, but nothing breaks without one.
- **A chore loses its only gate, and this must be decided deliberately or the shape is wrong.** `routeAfterTriage` sends `triage:chore` straight to `planning` ([`pipeline.ts:421`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)), skipping requirements entirely. Today the plan gate is the single human touch a chore gets; with `planning` ungated, a chore would go triage → planning → execution → a pull request with **no gate at any point**. The pull request is still a human's to merge, so nothing ships unreviewed — but a chore would be built before anyone was asked. The implementing phase must either route chores through `breakdown` too, or say plainly that a chore's judgement moves to its pull request.
- **The contradiction with `timone-execute` must be resolved in the same phase, not after it.** [`.claude/skills/timone-execute/SKILL.md:54`](../../.claude/skills/timone-execute/SKILL.md) — gate 1 — refuses "a phase file not stamped `Approved for execution`", naming a file with no `Status` line at all as covered. Under D1 every per-chunk phase file is exactly that, so execution would refuse every chunk. **What has to change:** gate 1 stops keying on the stamp and keys on the *breakdown's* approval — the phase file's `Status` line becomes a lifecycle marker (`Complete — see <report>`) rather than a gate trace, and the thing execution refuses to start without is a chunk listed in an `Approved` breakdown. `process.md` stage 5's gate column ([`process.md:15`](../../process.md)) and its Status-lifecycle paragraph ([`process.md:37`](../../process.md)) carry the same rule and move with it. Until all three agree, [ADR-0014](0014-artifact-first-gates.md)'s "write first, then gate" is unchanged in substance — it just gates a different artifact.
- **The daemon acquires the ability to merge, and the instruction that forbids it acquires a scope.** `src/git.ts` gains its first write-to-another-branch primitive (or the merge goes through the GitHub adapter), and [`prompts.ts:429`](../../src/daemon/prompts.ts)'s "**Never merge** — merging is the human's act" must be narrowed to say what it has always meant: never merge *a chunk's pull request*. A blanket rule that the machine itself now breaks in one place is a rule the next reader will not trust.
- **The rhythm becomes one gate for a whole initiative.** After this, a five-chunk milestone is approved once at `breakdown` and then judged five times as pull requests — which is precisely what [ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md) chose, and it is worth stating that this record is where that choice stops being reversible cheaply. If the shape turns out to be too little oversight at real size, the thing to revisit is [ADR-0026](0026-a-ticket-is-a-conversation-a-run-is-a-chunk.md)'s rhythm, not this mechanism.
- **What does not change:** gate parsing and the rule that the ticket is the sole write-path for gate decisions ([ADR-0012](0012-conversation-channels.md)); `readGate`'s two outcomes ([`pipeline.ts:503`](../../src/daemon/pipeline.ts)); [ADR-0016](0016-review-remediation-rides-the-verify-fix-shape.md); settledness and `TERMINAL` ([ADR-0029](0029-a-chunk-advances-only-on-success.md)); and the human-facing vocabulary — nobody types the word "breakdown" or a chunk number to make any of this happen.
